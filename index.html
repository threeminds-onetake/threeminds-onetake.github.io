About the Show

Three minds walk into a bar. One knows how psychopaths operate, the other understands the brain, the third has been studying business decisions, both academically and in practice. And happens to be married to another.

Three Minds. One Take. is the podcast for executives and senior leaders who've sat through enough corporate theatre to appreciate a straight answer. We talk leadership, strategy, and the decisions nobody writes case studies about - yet.

Real access. Honest conversation. All three have opinions. The only question is who gets to speak loudest.

01
CEO Advisor & Exec Team Specialist

Lauren J Kester

Lauren has spent her career in the room where it happens - like a business version of Alexander Hamilton. And like the musical, usually with the door closed. She advises CEOs and executive teams on the things that determine whether a leadership team flies or quietly implodes: alignment, dynamics, and the gap between perception and reality, examined like proper forensic witness testimony. Not surprisingly, Lauren used to study serial killers before she pivoted to CEOs. Whoever guesses her favourite killer can expect a prize.

She brings real access and genuine curiosity to every conversation. She is not here to validate your strategy or your thinking. She is here to make it better. And probably have a good laugh about it in the process.

"The most useful thing I can do is tell you the truth - and then help you figure out what to do with it."

02
Academic & Founder, Executive Brain Institute

Dr Michael Netzley

Michael is a professor, a researcher, and the founder of the Executive Brain Institute - which means he has spent a significant portion of his career studying what makes leaders effective, and a significant portion of this podcast politely explaining why the other two are wrong.

He arrives with frameworks, citations, and a whisky. He will tell you what the research says even when the room would prefer a simpler answer. Occasionally, he gets to be right. Often, he is the reason Lauren and Boy agree on something.

"The evidence says otherwise. But do go on - I find it useful data."
Neuroscience of Leadership Executive Performance Research & Evidence Academic Rigour
Cold lager - unpretentious, effective
03
Strategist

Boy W.J. Kester

Boy is a strategist who happens to have studied this topic both academically and in practice, spending over 20 years with some of the largest companies in the world - by headcount or by revenue. He insisted on adding those two specs.

He has a talent for seeing through complexity and saying the quiet part out loud, while being allergic to bullshit. Not afraid to play the culture card (he is Dutch and thinks that gives him a free pass to be blunt), he works with organisations on the cusp of shaping their future - or helping them get there. He is one of the few people truly interested in business history. Don't get him started.

He also has the additional professional hazard of being married to his co-host, which gives him a unique perspective on executive dynamics and an extremely thick skin. Michael has noted that this makes certain conversations "ethnographically fascinating" and frightening at the same time.

"Everyone in the room already knows. Someone has to say it out loud."
